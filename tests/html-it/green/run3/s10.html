Architecture review proposal

Appointment reminders: move to a queue

We recommend option B: send appointment reminders through a RabbitMQ queue instead of the nightly cron job, because it is the only option that fixes late reminders, manual retries and the 02:00 load peak while keeping patient data in our hosting. Lena (tech lead) decides at the architecture review; if she chooses B, work starts in sprint 47.

The problem today

Reminders are sent by a nightly cron job in the core service. Three things go wrong:

  • 6 % of reminders arrive late.
  • Failed reminders are retried by hand (KT-955 manual retries of reminders).
  • All sending happens in one batch, which creates a load peak at 02:00.

The 6 % figure is the headline. Patients who get a late reminder are more likely to miss the appointment.

A is cheap but leaves the peak. C is fast but brings a monthly bill and a compliance question. B costs the most effort once and solves both problems.

How option B works

The core service puts one message per reminder on a RabbitMQ queue. A sender takes messages off the queue at the pace it can handle, so the work no longer lands at 02:00 all at once; a failed send goes back on the queue and is retried automatically.

Back-pressure is the reason the 02:00 peak goes away. Retries come from the queue, not from a person.

Why not A or C

Option A is the cheapest at 3 days and removes the manual retries, but the cron job still sends everything in one batch, so the load peak at 02:00 stays.

Option C takes 1 sprint, but costs about 1,800 EUR per month, and patient data would leave our hosting. Under the security rule SEC-20 patient data stays in EU-hosted systems with a DPA, that requires a data processing agreement with the provider before any reminder is sent.

If someone asks about C: the agreement is a precondition, not a formality, and the monthly cost recurs.

What option B costs us

  • Effort: 2 sprints.
  • No new infrastructure: RabbitMQ is already in use by sms-service.
  • Operations needs an ops runbook for the reminder queue before go-live.

The runbook is the one new obligation. Who writes it is not decided yet.

Glossary

Back-pressure
A queue slows down or holds back work when the consumer is busy, instead of overloading it.
Cron job
A job that the operating system scheduler starts at fixed times, here once per night.
Data processing agreement (DPA)
A contract that binds an external company processing personal data on our behalf to data protection rules.
Ops runbook
A written guide for the operations team: how to monitor the service, what alarms mean and what to do when something fails.
RabbitMQ
A message broker: services put messages on queues and other services take them off, independently of each other.
