Patient portal team: usability test prototype

    Password reset prototype

    This clickable prototype of the patient portal's password reset is ready for the usability test on 6 November 2026. Testers go from the login screen to a new password and back to sign in; the moderator resets the flow or jumps to any screen with the controls beside it. Every email address leads to the same "Check your inbox" screen, so the portal never reveals whether an account exists, as the security rule SEC-31 never reveal whether an account exists requires.

    Prepared 3 November 2026 for the patient portal team and the moderator of the usability test. Open the file in a browser on the test laptop; it needs no network connection.

    Date to confirm: the test plan says "Thursday 2026-11-06", but 6 November 2026 is a Friday. Check with the test organiser whether the test is on Thursday 5 November or Friday 6 November 2026.

    What to try

    The main path has five screens, in this order:

    1. On the login screen, select "Forgot password?".
    2. Enter an email address and send the reset link.
    3. "Check your inbox" appears. "Resend email" stays disabled for 60 seconds, then becomes available.
    4. Open the link and set a new password. A checklist under the field shows live which rules the password meets: at least 12 characters, and not one of the last 5 passwords.
    5. A success screen confirms the change and leads back to the login screen.

    Two error paths are built in. An email address that has no account shows exactly the same "Check your inbox" screen as step 3, so a tester cannot tell the difference; this is intended and follows SEC-31 no account enumeration. A link older than 30 minutes shows "This link has expired" with a button that sends a new link and returns to "Check your inbox".

    What is not real

    • No email is sent and no account is checked. Any address that looks like an email address is accepted.
    • The tester's inbox is simulated: two dashed "Prototype only" buttons on the "Check your inbox" screen stand in for opening the email, once with a valid link and once with a link older than 30 minutes.
    • The portal has no password history here. The prototype treats five invented example passwords, listed in the moderator controls and in Rules the screens follow, as the tester's last 5 passwords.
    • "Sign in" on the login screen does nothing; signing in is not part of this test.
    • Nothing is saved. Moderator notes stay in this browser on this laptop only and are not shared with anyone; copy them into the test log before closing the file.

      How the flow fits together

      The flow has one branch point: what happens when the tester opens the link from the email. A link younger than 30 minutes leads to the new-password screen; an older one leads to "This link has expired", and sending a new link from there restarts the 60-second Resend wait on "Check your inbox". An unknown email address is not a branch at all: it lands on the same screen as a known one, with the same text, which is the point of SEC-31 no account enumeration.

      References

      1. SEC-31 The team's security rule "never reveal whether an account exists". It forbids screens or messages that let someone find out whether an email address has a portal account.       Glossary

      Account enumeration
      Finding out which email addresses have an account by watching how a system responds to them, for example a different message for unknown addresses.
      Deep link
      A link that opens the page directly at one place; here, #screen- followed by a screen name opens that screen of the prototype.
