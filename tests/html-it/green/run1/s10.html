Design proposal: appointment reminders

Appointment reminders: move sending to a queue

The team recommends Option B: send appointment reminders through a message queue on RabbitMQ instead of the nightly cron job. It removes late reminders and manual retries for about 2 sprints of work.

Decision needed from Lena (tech lead) at the architecture review on Friday 7 November 2026. If Option B is chosen, work starts in sprint 47.

Open with the recommendation and who decides. The rest of the deck is the evidence: the problem, the three options, how B works and what B costs us. The last slide is the decision.

Today one nightly job sends every reminder, and 6 % arrive late

The core service sends all appointment reminders from a single cron job that runs once a night at 02:00. Two problems follow from that design:

  • 6 % of reminders arrive late. Every reminder is sent in the same nightly window, which creates a load peak at 02:00.
  • Failed reminders are retried by hand. When a send fails, someone has to notice and resend it; see KT-955 not found in the provided material.

The 6 % figure and the 02:00 peak come from the proposal; it does not state the time zone of the 02:00 run. The ticket cited for the manual retries is mentioned on this slide, but its content was not available when this deck was made, so it is marked as unresolved.

Three options

A: keep the cron job, add retries

Effort: 3 days

Quick, and retries no longer need a person. The nightly load peak at 02:00 stays, so it does not fix the cause of late reminders.

B: queue on RabbitMQ Recommended

Effort: 2 sprints

Retries and back-pressure are built in. RabbitMQ already runs for sms-service. Needs an ops runbook.

C: external notification provider

Effort: 1 sprint, about 1,800 EUR per month

Patient data leaves our hosting, so the provider needs a data processing agreement under SEC-20 patient data stays in EU-hosted systems.

A is cheap but treats the symptom. C is fast to build but adds a monthly cost and a contract, and moves patient data outside our hosting. B costs the most build time and is the only option that addresses the load peak and the retries together.

How Option B works

Walk the arrows left to right. The point is that jobs wait in the queue instead of all firing at 02:00, and a failed send is redelivered by the broker instead of by a person.

What Option B commits us to

  • 2 sprints of build work, compared with 3 days for Option A and 1 sprint for Option C.
  • An ops runbook, so that operations staff can monitor the reminder queue and recover it when it fails.
  • No new infrastructure product: RabbitMQ is already in use by sms-service.
  • Patient data stays in our hosting, so no new data processing agreement is needed under SEC-20 patient data stays in EU-hosted systems.

The runbook is real extra work and should be counted in the 2 sprints or planned next to them; ask ops who owns it.

Glossary

Back-pressure
A consumer takes new work only as fast as it can finish it, so a burst waits in the queue instead of overloading the sender.
Cron job
A job that the operating system starts on a fixed schedule, here once every night at 02:00.
Data processing agreement
A contract that binds an outside company to process personal data only on our instructions and under data-protection law.
Message queue
A list of jobs held by a broker. Producers add jobs; consumers take them off and process them at their own pace.
RabbitMQ
An open-source message broker, already used by sms-service.
Runbook
A written procedure that operations staff follow to run, monitor and recover a system.
